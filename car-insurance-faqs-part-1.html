Car Insurance FAQs

Third-party cover is the minimum amount of car insurance you need in order to legally drive a car, motorcycle, van or other vehicle in the UK. Failure to properly insure a vehicle can result in a fine and even prison time.

Third-party insurance covers your passengers, as well as the driver, vehicle and property of the other party if you cause an accident.

If you drive a car that's registered, insured and taxed, you should carry your V5C registration certificate, insurance details and MOT certificate within the vehicle, although it's not a legal obligation.

Multiple factors cause car insurance prices to increase, many of which are out of your control. The main external factor is inflation, which causes the cost of labour and parts to increase.

You may see higher insurance premiums if you claim on your insurance, increase your annual mileage, change your job, or get a car that's more expensive to insure.

There are lots of factors that can impact the cost of your car insurance. These include:

  • Age
  • Driving experience
  • Past convictions or car accidents
  • No Claims Bonus
  • Expected annual mileage
  • Job title
  • Whether the vehicle is used for private, business or commercial use

You can cancel your car insurance before your renewal date, but you could be charged a cancellation fee of £50 or more. You'll also have to sacrifice that year's no-claims discount. If you're cancelling your car insurance in favour of a cheaper deal, make sure you weigh up the costs associated with cancellation first.

No, having fully comprehensive cover on your own vehicle does not mean you are insured to drive someone else's car.

You may be insured to drive someone else's vehicle if you have Driving Other Cars (DOC) cover included in your own policy, or if the other vehicle owner adds you as a named driver to their own insurance policy.

Neither of these will guarantee you'll have fully comprehensive cover when driving the other person's car, so check quotes carefully before selecting your insurance or adding optional extras.

It's generally thought that the best time to renew car insurance is between 20 and 27 days before your renewal date.

Any closer to your renewal date, insurers are likely to deem you higher risk and less responsible. Any earlier than 27 days before, you won't have as many insurance quotes to choose from, so you may have to settle for a higher premium.

While you technically can have two insurance policies on one car, it is only really beneficial if you're insuring two people on that same vehicle, and only if those two drivers carry different levels of risk. It could be fraudulent to claim on two separate policies for the same accident.
