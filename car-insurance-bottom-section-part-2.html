What is car insurance, and why do I need it?

Car insurance is a type of insurance policy that covers damage and/or injury caused to either yourself or other parties involved in a car accident. Who and what exactly is covered by the insurance will depend on who owns the policy and what level of cover they have.

As a driver in the UK, getting insured on the vehicle you drive is a legal requirement. It can also offer financial protection for you in the event of an accident.

The car insurance policy is used primarily by the vehicle owner to protect the car from losses caused due to fire, theft, natural disasters and accidents. However, if the car owner is found to have caused the accident, the insurance company can decline to pay for the damages.

Car insurance companies offer various insurance plans, from third-party to fully comprehensive insurance, so you can choose the right cover for you based on your vehicle and how often you drive.

Other types of car insurance

Depending on your circumstances, you may need a more specialised car insurance policy to suit your needs. Here are some of the other types of car insurance available.

Temporary

Temporary car insurance is for drivers who need short-term coverage for a vehicle they don't usually drive.

Pay-as-you-go

Pay-as-you-go car insurance means you'll pay less when you drive fewer miles. This can be a more cost-effective option if you're a low-mileage driver or drive infrequently.

Learner Driver

Learner driver insurance allows you to legally practice driving in someone else's car before you have your full driving licence.

Black Box

Black box insurance (or telematics insurance) can be a good idea for new and young drivers. Low-risk, responsible driving will help you unlock lower insurance premiums next time you renew.

Specialist

Specialist car insurance is for high-value, modified or classic cars, as well as high-risk drivers with previous driving convictions.

Electric

Electric car insurance will provide extra cover for your car battery, charging cables and other components of your EV.

Business

Business car insurance provides cover when driving a car that you use for work.

Commercial

Commercial car insurance provides cover when you are driving a vehicle that is necessary for your job. This applies to professions like taxi drivers, delivery van drivers and driving instructors.

Car insurance optional add-ons

You can add optional extras to your car insurance policy if you need additional cover. Here are some of the most popular insurance add-ons, but we haven't exhausted the list. If you require extra cover, factor this in when you compare car insurance quotes.

If you take out uninsured driver protection, your excess will be reimbursed and your no-claims discount protected if you're involved in an accident caused by an uninsured driver. Fully comprehensive cover often includes uninsured driver protection, but not all policies will.

No Claims Bonus (NCB) or No Claims Discount (NCD) protection is a car insurance add-on that allows you to make one or more claims on your car insurance a year without it affecting your no-claims bonus.

All UK car insurance policies include a minimum of third-party cover for driving in most European countries. If you want fully comprehensive cover while driving abroad, or are travelling outside of Europe, driving abroad cover is an optional add-on.

In the event you accidentally put the wrong fuel into your car, misfuelling cover can help you recoup the costs of draining, cleaning and repairing your car engine.

Adding lost car key insurance to your car policy can not only help you recoup your costs associated with losing your car key, it can also take out some of the stress and hassle. You can claim the cost of an emergency car key replacement, as well as alternative transport or hire car costs.

You may be able to get cheaper breakdown cover if you get it alongside your car insurance policy. Make sure you check to see if breakdown cover is cheaper when bundled with your insurance or bought separately.

Motor legal protection can help cover your legal costs if you're involved in a car accident that wasn't your fault and your regular car insurance doesn't cover the claim. This can include loss of earnings if you're in an accident that means you can no longer work, or compensation for damage to your car or belongings. Motor legal protection can also protect you in the event of a motoring prosecution.

If you claim on your car insurance, you will have to pay the pre-agreed insurance excess, and your insurance provider will cover the rest of your costs (up to your agreed limit).

So, if you have a £300 excess and you're making a £1,000 insurance claim, you'll pay the first £300 and your insurance provider will pay the remaining £700.

With an excess protection insurance add-on, you can get your excess reimbursed when you make a claim on your insurance.

Personal accident cover will pay out in the event of a life-changing, serious injury or death caused by a car accident. The definition of a "life-changing injury" can vary between providers, so make sure you check the policy wording.

Because personal accident cover is a 'no-fault' insurance add-on, you can claim and get a payout even if the accident was your fault. Personal accident cover is often included as standard in comprehensive car insurance policies. You may want to add it as an extra if your policy doesn't include this as standard.

Tips for bagging cheaper car insurance

These things may make your car insurance cheaper, but there are no guarantees. Weigh up your options before committing to any insurance product.

Don't assume the lowest level of cover is cheapest

While third-party insurance provides the least cover and comprehensive cover provides the most, it's not always the case that comprehensive is more expensive. Third-party car insurance is actually often more expensive than other levels of cover. Take some time to compare car insurance quotes to ensure you're getting the right cover to suit your needs.

1

Pay annually rather than monthly

Monthly car insurance payments usually come with extra interest. If you can afford to pay for your insurance annually, you'll avoid those extra charges. To ensure you can afford to pay in full when your insurance is up for renewal, you could set aside a portion each month throughout the year.

2

Build up your no-claims bonus

Drivers who don't claim on their car insurance may benefit from no-claims discounts when it's time to renew. This is because drivers who don't claim on their insurance are generally viewed as more responsible, less risky drivers. You could unlock discounts of as much as 60% if you build up multiple consecutive years without claiming on your insurance, even if you switch car insurance providers.

3

Consider a higher voluntary excess

By increasing your voluntary excess, you could lower your insurance premiums. This could save you money if you don't need to claim on your insurance. However, if you do need to make a claim, you are agreeing to pay more money upfront. Only set a voluntary excess you're confident you can afford to pay in the event of a claim.

4

Consider multi-car insurance

If your household has more than one car, it could be more cost-effective to get a multi-car policy instead of separate ones. This is because many insurance companies offer multi-car discounts.

5

Don't pay for extras if you don't need them

Choosing the right additional cover for you can help you save money in the long run if you end up making a claim. However, if you're paying for extras you don't need, you could actually be wasting money. Comprehensive insurance covers much more than third-party or third-party, fire and theft insurance. You may find that fully comp has everything you need without adding additional cover.

6

Consider adding another named driver

You can add another named driver to your car insurance policy, which will usually mean they have the same level of insurance to drive your car as you do. Doing this can reduce your insurance premium if they're deemed a lower-risk driver, although this isn't guaranteed. Only add someone as a named driver if they will actually be driving your car.
